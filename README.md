# Vendor Performance Analysis

An end-to-end data analytics project analyzing vendor and sales performance for a retail/wholesale business, using **SQL, Python, and Power BI**.

## Business Problem

Effective inventory and sales management is critical for optimizing profitability in the retail and wholesale industry. Companies need to ensure they are not incurring losses due to inefficient pricing, poor inventory turnover, or vendor dependency.

The goal of this analysis was to:
- Identify underperforming brands that require promotional or pricing adjustments
- Determine top vendors contributing to sales and gross profit
- Analyze the impact of bulk purchasing on unit costs
- Assess inventory turnover to reduce holding costs and improve efficiency
- Investigate the profitability variance between high-performing and low-performing vendors

## Tools & Tech Stack

- **Python** (Pandas, NumPy, SQLAlchemy, Matplotlib, Seaborn, SciPy) — data ingestion, cleaning, EDA, hypothesis testing
- **SQL** (SQLite) — data storage and query logic (CTEs, joins, aggregations)
- **Power BI** — interactive dashboard and visualization
- **VS Code** — development environment (Jupyter notebooks + Python scripts)

## Project Workflow

1. **Data Cleaning** (`Cleaning.ipynb`) — Loaded and cleaned the raw data: handled missing values, fixed data types, removed extra spaces from categorical columns, and did initial exploration before building the summary table.

2. **Data Merging & Summary Table** (`get_vendor_summary.py`) — Wrote a SQL query using CTEs to merge purchase, sales, and freight data into a single `vendor_sales_summary` table, then ingested the cleaned result into a SQLite database. Engineered new metrics:
   - Gross Profit
   - Profit Margin
   - Stock Turnover
   - Sales-to-Purchase Ratio

3. **Exploratory Data Analysis & Business Questions** (`vendor_DA.ipynb`) — Explored distributions, identified top vendors/brands by sales and purchase contribution, visualized vendor concentration (Pareto and donut charts), and answered the core business questions driving this analysis.

4. **Statistical Analysis** — Compared profit margins between top-performing and low-performing vendors (split by purchase quantile thresholds) using a two-sample t-test to test for statistically significant differences.

5. **Power BI Dashboard** — Built an interactive dashboard (using `vendor_sales_summary.csv`) featuring:
   - KPI cards (Total Sales, Total Purchase, Gross Profit, Profit Margin, Unsold Capital)
   - Purchase Contribution % by Top 10 Vendors (donut chart)
   - Top Vendors/Brands by Sales (bar charts)
   - Low Performing Vendors
   - Low Performing Brands (scatter plot, flagging low-sales/high-margin brands using percentile-based DAX logic)

## Key Insights & Recommendations

- **Re-evaluate pricing** for low-sales, high-margin brands to boost sales volume without sacrificing profitability
- **Diversify vendor partnerships** to reduce dependency on a few suppliers and mitigate supply chain risk
- **Leverage bulk purchasing advantages** to maintain competitive pricing while optimizing inventory management
- **Optimize slow-moving inventory** by adjusting purchase quantities, launching clearance sales, or revising storage strategies
- **Enhance marketing and distribution** for low-performing vendors to drive higher sales volumes without compromising profit margins

Implementing these recommendations can help the business achieve sustainable profitability, mitigate risk, and improve overall operational efficiency.
